The mark.

Two houses, one bolt. The houses are the opposing picks in a family-vacation argument; the bolt down the middle is the battle. The whole thing is the deal we offer: stop arguing in four group chats, settle it in one place.

Left house
One half of the argument. Teal — #10C8D2. The one with the chimney — taller, slightly more cluttered profile. Always on the left, always paired with the BATTLE half of the wordmark on the opposite side (crossed scheme).
Right house
The other half. Rose — #FF6C51. Cleaner single-apex roof, no chimney. The two houses are deliberately different silhouettes — they share the window grid, baseline, and proportions, but interlock around the bolt rather than mirroring. Rivals that belong to the same family without being the same house.
The bolt
The fight. A jagged negative-space gap between the two houses. Reads as lightning, as a tear, as the moment of decision. Never fill it in; never soften it.
Windows
Square 2×2-grid cutouts. Crisp rectangles — do not round, do not bevel. Cleaned up from earlier squiggly path data so they hold up at 16px.

STAYBATTLE wordmark.

Wordmark + tagline.

When the tagline SETTLE THE VACATION ARGUMENT needs to live with the mark, use one of the baked *-tagline.svg variants. The two variants ship with different type: the banner uses Hammersmith One (a wide slab sans, embedded as SVG paths — no webfont — with extra inter-glyph spacing so the line spans the wordmark width), while the square uses IBM Plex Sans as a live <text> element with system fallbacks. Treat the banner as the canonical typographic lockup; the square is a lighter, system-font caption.

Voice & tone.

Written like a friend who's been through it. Conversational, mildly profane when it lands a punchline, allergic to corporate vocabulary. We're not a SaaS, not a startup, not an MVP — just the tool.

01

Specific over general.

"Six people, twelve Airbnbs, four group texts" lands. "Streamline group decisions" doesn't.

02

First-person, not third.

"My family cannot pick a vacation rental" — not "Families have difficulty selecting accommodations."

03

Mild profanity earns its keep.

"Trip is in three weeks. Cool." works. Save harder words for genuine frustration.

04

Concrete metaphors only.

"Meeting table, not the storefront." Visual, physical, real. Skip "platform", "solution", "enabler".

✓ DO
"Drop every candidate Airbnb URL into one place. Vote together. Argue in the open. Settle it on the map."

Imperative verbs, plain nouns, no abstraction. The reader can see themselves doing each step.

✕ DON'T
"Empower your group to optimize the consensus-building process for accommodation selection through structured deliberation."

Generic SaaS register. Three Latinate words in a row, no concrete image, sounds like every other landing page.

✓ DO
"StayBattle is the meeting table, not the storefront."

Names what we are AND what we aren't, in one sentence, with a visual metaphor.

✕ DON'T
"StayBattle is the leading open-source collaborative decision platform for vacation rental groups."

"Leading" is a lie if unproven. "Platform" is dead language. Describes a category, not a product.

Motion.

Three rules. One: motion is communication, not decoration — every animation must say "this just changed" or "this is interactive." Two: respect prefers-reduced-motion. Three: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1) is the house easing curve.

Hover lift

Cards and buttons translate -2 to -4px on hover with a soft glow added beneath. Just enough to feel responsive.

.15s · ease-out

Scroll reveal

Sections fade up 24px when they enter the viewport. Staggered by 100ms when there's a group of cards.

.8s · cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)

Shimmer

One headline accent ("For real.") gets a slow gradient wipe. Once is delightful; on every heading it's TV static.

6s · ease-in-out · infinite

Live dot pulse

Status indicators (voting open, live demo) emit a slow glow pulse. ~4s cycle, never strobing.

4s · ease-in-out · infinite
